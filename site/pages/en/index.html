[01] the problem

An agent runs
commands on
your machine.

blind[01]

It runs what it writes.

A model reads a file that tells it what to do, and does it — with your rights, your keys, your network.

Every command in macOS Seatbelt or Linux bubblewrap, the network through a proxy that asks.

locked[02]

One vendor, one model.

The tool decides which model you may use, and what it costs you.

Two dozen providers, local models, your ChatGPT plan — and the cost of every turn in the footer.

fragile[03]

A change you cannot take back.

An edit too many, a run that dies halfway, and an afternoon is gone.

A snapshot before every change, /steps to put files back, and journals that let a lost run resume.

cut[04]

It guesses what it was not shown.

Long output is cut off, and the model fills the gap with what sounds right.

Nothing is ever cut: the whole result waits in a store the model can page through.

Glut is for people who let an agent work in their real projects, on their real machines — and want to know, at every step, what it may do, what it did, and how to take it back.

01 / 04

The sandbox

Every command runs in the operating system's own sandbox — Seatbelt on macOS, bubblewrap with Glut's seccomp filter on Linux. It writes your project and the usual caches, nothing else. It reads no .env, and leaves git hooks, .cargo/config.toml or .vscode/tasks.json alone at any depth. Where no sandbox can run, every command asks you first.

02 / 04

The gate

Reading is free; changing asks — or not, in the mode you choose. A command that only reads runs at once; rm -rf, git push and sudo always ask. "Always" means the scope you pick, never one Glut widens.

03 / 04

The snapshot

Before every change, Glut keeps your files as they were. /steps puts them back as they were before any step; /diff shows the session's changes file by file, and a hunk can go back alone.

04 / 04

The masks

A key shown by accident never reaches the model. Secrets are masked where a result enters the conversation; your provider keys live in the Keychain, bound to their host.
